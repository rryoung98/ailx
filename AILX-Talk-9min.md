**\[0:00 — 50 sec\]**

## Cold open — put the room in the test

*Do not introduce the project. Make them do it first.*

**Screen**

One QR code. No title, no logo, no bullet points. Black slide.

**Say**

“Phones out. Scan this. Ten images. You have sixty seconds to tell me which ones are real.”

*Then stop talking. Allow sixty seconds of silence. Do not fill it.*

**Watch**

- Start the deck before you say anything else. Every second you spend explaining is a second the timer eats.
- If the room is slow to scan, say the short URL out loud once and move on.

**\[0:50 — 50 sec\]**

## The result, live

*Their own number is the argument. Nothing you say beats it.*

**Screen**

Live histogram of the room's scores as they land. One number large: how many people got all ten.

**Say**

“That's this room. \[Read the number.\]”

“In a study of two thousand people, one in a thousand got everything right. Confidence stayed above sixty percent whether people were right or wrong. You just reproduced it in a room of people who work on this for a living.”

“There are hundreds of benchmarks for AI systems. There is not one for us.”

**Watch**

- Do not soften it. The room being wrong is the entire premise. They will enjoy it if you do not apologise.
- Have a fallback number on the slide in case fewer than ten people submit.

**\[1:40 — 50 sec\]**

## What AILX is

*Describe four tracks in forty words. Then stop.*

**Screen**

Four cards, one line each. T1 Creative Build · T2 Authenticity Discrimination · T3 AI-Assisted Reasoning · T4 Generative Direction. 100 points each.

**Say**

“An examination for people, not models. Four tracks, a hundred points each.”

“Build something and ship it. Tell real from fake. Solve a hard problem with an AI that is lying to you. Direct a model to communicate one specific idea.”

“It is not a quiz. You do the work, and we score what you produced.”

**Watch**

Do not explain the scoring here. Explain it at 5:45, after they have seen the exam.

**\[2:30 — 45 sec\]**

## Demo 1 — the replay

*Show them the tell they looked straight past. This is the best moment in the talk.*

**Screen**

Their own deck, replayed card by card. For each: what they called it, what it was, how confident they were. On the synthetic ones, the artefact circled, and the signed Content Credential naming the generator.

**Say**

“Here is card four. Seventy percent of you called it real. Look at the shadow under the left hand and the shadow behind the chair. They point in different directions.”

“And here is why we can prove the answer key. We generate every synthetic item in this exam. Each item ships with a signed credential naming the model that made it. Your own lawyer can verify that without trusting a word I say.”

**Watch**

- Pick one card and go deep. Three shallow cards is worse than one card they will remember.
- Choose a physics violation, not a hand or garbled text — those tells are dead and someone in the room knows it.

**\[3:15 — 45 sec\]**

## Demo 2 — the planted error

*Nobody else does this. State it directly.*

**Screen**

A split transcript. Same AI, same false claim, two candidates. One accepted it and built an argument on top. One challenged it and went back to the source.

**Say**

“In track three we seed the assistant with things that are wrong. They are plausible, well-written, and wrong.”

“This candidate took the number and built three paragraphs on it. This one stopped, went back to page forty of the source, and found the real figure.”

“That is twenty-five points. No model judged anything. It is arithmetic on what the person did.”

**Watch**

Expect a question about whether it is fair to lie to candidates. The short answer is that the tools lie to them every day. The exam tells them in advance that some outputs will be wrong.

**\[4:00 — 60 sec\]**

## Demo 3 — the work, and how it gets judged

*Show real artefacts. Show two at a time, not a grid.*

**Screen**

- **0:00–0:35** — Two websites side by side, full bleed, no names. One question underneath: *which would you put your own name on?*
- **0:35–1:00** — Track four: the brief, the finished image set, and the prompt log open beside it.

**Say**

“A model does not score aesthetics. On comparative aesthetic judgement, the best frontier model scores twenty-six percent. A human expert scores sixty-nine.”

“So we ask people. They compare two at a time, blind. Each person makes twelve comparisons in fifteen minutes. Those comparisons produce the ranking.”

“Track four applies the same method to generation. You get three final renders. Not unlimited. Knowing when to stop is part of the skill.”

**Watch**

If you show a grid of thumbnails, you have made the point that models are bad at scaling comparisons and then made the same mistake yourself. Two at a time.

**\[5:00 — 45 sec\]**

## Demo 4 — what you take home

*The report is the product for a participant. Show one screen and let it sit.*

**Screen**

One diagnostic report. Four track scores, then the three things nobody has ever told them: their response bias, their calibration curve against the diagonal, and which planted errors got past them.

**Say**

“Everyone gets this, whatever they score.”

“This line says you lean toward believing things are real. Seventy-one percent of your errors were synthetic media you accepted.”

“And this curve shows your confidence against your accuracy. The gap between them is worth fixing. Almost nobody knows their own number.”

**\[5:45 — 75 sec\]**

## Why it holds up

*Answer three objections before they are raised. Move fast. Do not hedge.*

**Objection 1 — “This already exists”**

“Partly. GLAT and AICOS are real instruments and they are properly validated. They are multiple-choice knowledge tests. Nobody scores a person actually doing the work.”

“So the claim is narrow, and I will state it precisely: the first cross-nationally normed, annually re-versioned, task-performance-based AI literacy examination for adults. Every word there is doing work.”

“One caveat. The exam runs in three languages. The first population number will cover two countries, the United States and the United Kingdom, because those are the two where a probability sample can be bought. Japan and Korea have no panel to rent. The fieldwork has to be commissioned. That costs another one to one point six million dollars we have not raised. Until we raise it, nothing we publish describes adults in Japan or Korea.”

“And if you are about to say PISA — yes. PISA 2029 fields Media and AI Literacy. Its draft framework puts about half the assessment time on analyse, evaluate and create. It is performance-based too. It tests fifteen-year-olds, once every three years, starting 2029. We test adults, every year. Nobody else is building that.”

**Objection 2 — “You are using AI to grade AI literacy”**

“We deliberately score each track differently. Track two is arithmetic on response data. Thirty-five points of track three are arithmetic on behaviour. Humans judge aesthetics.”

“If someone publishes a paper tomorrow demolishing LLM-as-judge, it costs us forty-five points out of four hundred. It does not cost us the exam.”

**Objection 3 — “Where does the content come from”**

“We generate it. The public deepfake datasets are research-licensed and built on faces nobody consented to. They test 2019 technology. Detectors score eighty-seven percent on a 2018 model and nineteen on Imagen 4.”

“One of them would be a criminal exposure to hold on a laptop in Seoul. We do not touch them. The exclusion list is in the methodology annex.”

**\[7:00 — 50 sec\]**

## Why it survives next year

*This is the part that makes it a programme rather than an event.*

**Screen**

Two numbers, large: **87%** detector accuracy on a 2018 generator, **19%** on Imagen 4.

**Say**

“A benchmark built on last year's models measures history. The whole instrument is data, not code. We create a new form every year. Each form is versioned and signed. A secure block of items never rotates, so we can compare results across years.”

“The export matters as much as the score. A ministry gets a distribution for a population it currently cannot measure. A lab gets a calibrated human baseline on the same tasks it uses to evaluate its models. It does not have that now.”

**\[7:50 — 50 sec\]**

## The ask

*Name three specific things and stop.*

**Screen**

Three lines. Nothing else.

**Say**

“One. This room, as the first cohort. A first cohort across three countries is a small, unrepeatable sample. We would report it as calibration, not certification.”

“Two. Item writers in Japanese and Korean. Nothing usable exists to source in either language, so we are writing the items. Native speakers must write them. This is the real constraint, not money.”

“Three. A legal read from each ministry. We also need one lab to co-sign the judge protocol before we run it rather than after.”

“Infrastructure for the pilot is about seven hundred and fifty dollars a month. That was never the hard part.”

**\[8:40 — 20 sec\]**

## Close

**Screen**

Back to the histogram from 0:50. Same slide, no new text.

**Say**

“That number in the first minute is why this exists. This room is not bad at it. Nobody has ever told you how good you are. Next year the answer will be different.”

**Watch**

End on the slide, not on a thank-you. Leave the number on screen while questions start.

## If it goes wrong

| Failure                              | What you do                                                                                                                                                               |
|--------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Wifi dies before the cold open       | Run the deck as slides from local files and have the room call out real or fake by show of hands. Count it yourself. The point survives; only the live histogram is lost. |
| Under ten submissions                | Read the pre-loaded reference number instead. The line is: “this is what a room like this normally does.” Do not say the sample was too small.                            |
| Demo environment will not load       | Every demo block has a recorded fallback clip. Play it and narrate over the top. Never debug on stage.                                                                    |
| Someone challenges the “first” claim | You have already conceded it at 5:45. Repeat the narrowed sentence, name GLAT and AICOS, and say you would rather be precisely right than loudly first.                   |
| Running long at 7:00                 | Cut the re-versioning block to one sentence: “new form every year, secure anchor items so years stay comparable.” Never cut the ask.                                      |

## Cut list, in order

If you are over time, cut in this order. Remove each section cleanly. Do not compress it.

1.  **Demo 4, the diagnostic report** — 45 seconds. Fold one line of it into the close.
2.  **Objection 3, item sourcing** — 25 seconds. Only cut this if there are no ministry lawyers in the room.
3.  **Track four in demo 3** — 25 seconds. Show only the two websites.
4.  **The re-versioning block** — down to one sentence, 40 seconds saved.

Never cut the cold open, the replay, the planted error, or the ask. Those four make the talk.

## What to have ready before you walk up

- QR code slide, and the short URL memorised so you can say it out loud.
- A pre-loaded fallback number for the cold open, in case submissions are thin.
- One chosen card for the replay — a shadow or reflection inconsistency, not hands or text.
- Recorded fallback clips for all four demo blocks.
- The narrowed positioning sentence, word for word: *the first cross-nationally normed, annually re-versioned, task-performance-based AI literacy examination for adults.* Followed, unprompted, by which countries the norm covers: US and UK first, Japan and Korea when the fieldwork is funded.
- Two numbers you will be asked for and should not have to look up: **80 of 400** points exposed to model judging by design — **180 of 400** as the system is built today, because the unbuilt gates and the unbuilt human panel fall back to the judge path — and **\$750** a month for the pilot. (`pointsByResolution()` in `packages/core/src/allocation.ts` is the source; AILX-Spec-2026.1.md §04 states both columns.)

## Questions you will get

| Question                                      | Answer, in one breath                                                                                                                                                                       |
|-----------------------------------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Is a first cohort enough?                     | No, and we say so. About thirty people calibrates items to within one logit, which is enough to rank difficulty and cull bad items. Certification needs two hundred and fifty. Year one reports percentiles. |
| Does this just measure English?               | Every item ships in all three languages with recorded translation provenance, and we analyse difficulty by language from the first cohort. If there is a language effect we publish it.     |
| When do we get a number for Japan or Korea?   | When the fieldwork is funded. Neither country has a probability panel to rent, so each needs a commissioned sample — one to one point six million dollars on top of the US and UK wave. The exam is trilingual today; the statistic is not.                                              |
| What stops someone gaming the aesthetic vote? | Self-exclusion is mandatory, work is anonymous, and per-rater bias is estimated and corrected. That correction alone removes about ninety-five percent of the recoverable error.            |
| Where does the data live?                     | Tokyo. Named transfers disclosed in a single table. No face capture, no webcam proctoring, ever — that would pull us into Korea's high-impact AI category.                                  |
| Why should a lab care?                        | Human sensitivity numbers on 2026-current synthetic media, and human rejection rates for planted model errors. Neither exists anywhere at this quality.                                     |
