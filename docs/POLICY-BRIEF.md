# POLICY-BRIEF.md — what we can say to a policy audience today

TEN-22. Written 2026-09-02. This is a talking-points pack for conversations with government and
intergovernmental staff. It is not a pitch deck. It asks only for a conversation.

**The sequencing rule.** Do not lead with the ask. The judging pipeline does not exist. The
`scores` table is empty. The operational item bank was public in this repository until
2026-08-30. A national statistic pitched on a demo-scored instrument gets dismissed once and
never considered again. Lead with the problem and the method. The number comes later, or
not at all.

## 2. The policy hook, and how it changed in July 2026

### 2.1 Article 4 exists, applies now, and was softened six weeks ago

The AI Act obligation on AI literacy is Article 4 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689. It applies to
providers **and** deployers. It has applied since **2 February 2025** because Article 113(a) puts
Chapter I on that date. VERIFIED at EUR-Lex.

Most summaries still quote the old text. **Regulation (EU) 2026/1744** (the
Digital Omnibus on AI, 8 July 2026, in force mid-July 2026) replaced Article 4. VERIFIED at
`https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng`.

Old text:

> "Providers and deployers of AI systems shall take measures to ensure, to their best extent, a
> sufficient level of AI literacy of their staff..."

Text in force today:

> "Providers and deployers of AI systems shall take measures to support the development of AI
> literacy of their staff and other persons dealing with the operation and use of AI systems on
> their behalf... This obligation does not require providers or deployers to guarantee any
> specific level of AI literacy of any individual."

Recital 8 of the Omnibus explains that stringent obligations "would not be suitable for all
types of providers and deployers" and create "an additional compliance burden, particularly for
smaller enterprises". The same recital names frameworks a compliant actor may use: DigComp
and the AI Literacy Framework for Primary and Secondary Education.

**Say this accurately or not at all.** Anyone in an EU digital-policy job knows the Omnibus
happened. Quoting the 2024 wording as current marks us as someone reading blog posts.

### 2.2 No EU fine is attached to Article 4

Article 99(3), (4) and (5) list the provisions carrying EU-level administrative fines. Article 4
is in none of them. VERIFIED against the consolidated Article 99, including the point (da) the
Omnibus inserted. Article 99(1) does reach Article 4. It requires Member States to lay down
penalties that are "effective, proportionate and dissuasive".

The Commission's AI Literacy Q&A says national market surveillance authorities "will start
supervising and enforcing the rules as of 2 August 2026". One answer earlier, the same page says
"from 3 August 2026". Both spellings are live on the page today. Know that before someone corrects
us on the date.

### 2.3 The Commission says Article 4 does not oblige anyone to measure

There are two separate claims. Only the first is the Commission's. **The Commission's claim:** the
duty does not require measurement. **Ours:** we checked the three places an official measure
could sit and found none. Commission AI Office, "AI Literacy - Questions & Answers",
`https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/ai-literacy-questions-answers`, accessed
2026-09-02. VERIFIED.

> "Article 4 of the AI Act does not entail an obligation to measure the knowledge of AI of
> employees."

> "There is no need for a certificate. Organisations can keep an internal record of trainings
> and/or other guiding initiatives."

Three routes could have carried a measure. None did when we checked them on 2026-09-02:

- **The living repository of AI literacy practices.** More than 40 initiatives, collected from AI
  Pact pledgers and an open survey. The page says copying them "does not automatically grant
  presumption of compliance with Article 4". It is a catalogue of trainings.
- **The AI Pact.** The pledge asks signatories to commit to "Promoting AI awareness and literacy
  among staff". No scale, no test.
- **CEN-CENELEC.** Standardisation request C(2023) 3215 lists ten deliverables, from risk
  management to conformity assessment. The word "literacy" appears zero times in the annexes.
  The 2023 request therefore mandates no AI-literacy standard. COULD NOT VERIFY: the current JTC 21 work
  programme, which could contain an informative deliverable the request never asked for. Say "the
  standardisation request contains no literacy deliverable", not "no standard exists".

**The one thing to watch.** New Article 4(3) requires the AI Board to "adopt recommendations,
taking into account European competence frameworks... including by setting out common objectives".
As of 2026-09-02 no such recommendation is published. The Q&A describes it in the future
tense. If the Board attaches common objectives to Article 4, rewrite section 5 that week.

### 2.4 Adjacent instruments describe competences and stop there

- **Council of Europe, CETS No. 225, Article 20.** Each Party "shall encourage and promote
  adequate digital literacy and digital skills for all segments of the population". No
  instrument, no reporting metric. VERIFIED on an official-text mirror; `coe.int` blocked the
  fetch.
- **UNESCO AI competency framework for students (2024), §5.8.** "few attempts have been made to
  develop these sorts of instruments for assessing comprehensive AI competencies cross-cutting
  multiple progression levels." The same section tells implementers to construct their own
  assessment system. Quote it as "few attempts", not as "none". VERIFIED on a full-text mirror.
- **UNESCO AI competency framework for teachers (2024), §5.5.** Gives one worked example and
  prints its grading criteria as "(To be specified in accordance with the adapted learning
  objectives and the type of the assessment items)". VERIFIED on a mirror.

### 2.5 One official assessment is being built. Name it before someone else does.

**PISA 2029 Media and Artificial Intelligence Literacy (MAIL).** The first-draft framework is
public. It contains five competences, task descriptors, difficulty drivers and an example task.
It recommends that "half of the assessment time is allocated to 'analyse and evaluate' and
'create'". It is performance-based, internationally comparable and nationally representative. VERIFIED on a
mirror of the OECD draft; `oecd.org` blocked direct fetch.

It tests 15-year-olds in the PISA 2029 cycle. **We could not verify the reporting date.** OECD's
project page refused every fetch. A second reviewer who read it reports results expected in
December 2031. Do not give a year in a meeting until someone opens that page. The
OECD and European Commission **AILit** framework informs it. The PISA Governing Board declassified AILit on 8 April 2026. It is
the framework named in the Digital Omnibus recital. AILit contains no test of its own.

**IEA ICILS 2028** is not a second AI assessment, and calling it one will get us corrected. It
reports computer and information literacy plus computational thinking for Grade 8 students. It
works AI into those constructs and the questionnaires. Pilot March 2026, field test March to May 2027,
main survey 2028, report 2029, 34 education systems interested. Whether a separate AI scale is
planned: COULD NOT VERIFY.

Neither covers adults. Neither is connected to the Article 4 duty.

### 2.6 National strategies

- **United States, Executive Order 14277** (23 April 2025) on AI education for American youth. The
  words "assess", "assessment" and "measure" appear zero times. It creates the Presidential

  VERIFIED at govinfo.gov.
- **China, MOE guidelines for general AI education in primary and secondary schools (2025).**
  Requires a **school-based** evaluation index system over four dimensions, using performance

  comprehensive quality evaluation. No national test, no common scale. VERIFIED on a mirror PDF.
- Finland, Singapore, UAE, Korea, India, Japan, Estonia and the UK: COULD NOT VERIFY. Say nothing
  about them.

## 3. The measurement gap, and the exact wording that survives a check

### 3.1 The population-scale instruments are self-report

Every widely cited AI-literacy scale asks people to rate themselves. VERIFIED from the published
papers:

| Instrument | What it is | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| MAILS (Carolus et al. 2023) | Self-assessment, 11-point Likert | "we generated 56 items for the self-assessments"; "rate their own abilities using an 11-point Likert scale (0-10)". doi:10.1016/j.chbah.2023.100014 |
| SNAIL (Laupichler, Aster et al. 2023) | 31-item online questionnaire, Delphi item pool, no key | doi:10.1016/j.chbr.2023.100338 |
| AILQ (Ng et al. 2024) | "a 32-item self-reported questionnaire", 363 Hong Kong secondary students | doi:10.1111/bjet.13411 |

The MAILS authors defend the choice on the record: "it is, thus, reasonable to resort to a
self-report questionnaire to measure AI literacy". They are not hiding anything. They measure
self-perceived competence. That is different from what a person can do.

### 3.2 The performance-based tests exist, on students

AILIT (doi:10.1016/j.caeai.2023.100165) is "a validated multiple-choice test" for German
university students. Its abstract says existing instruments "rely primarily on
self-assessment". The short form AILIT-S (doi:10.1016/j.chbah.2025.100176) is 10 items tested on 1,465
university students in Germany, the UK and the US. Convenience samples, no weights, no population
frame. VERIFIED.

### 3.3 The exception, and the version of our claim that survives it

**Pew Research Center, "Public Awareness of Artificial Intelligence in Everyday Activities",
15 February 2023.** 11,004 US adults on the American Trends Panel, recruited by "national, random
sampling of residential addresses", weighted to the US adult population. Six questions with
correct answers. "30% of Americans correctly answered all six questions". Mean 3.7 out of 6.
VERIFIED.

It is also scaled. A first draft of this file was wrong about that. Pew's methodology page
reports Cronbach's alpha of 0.83 for the six items. It reports an exploratory factor analysis whose first
factor explains 91% of the shared variance. It also reports a two-parameter logistic IRT model with
item-level discrimination and difficulty. VERIFIED at
`https://www.pewresearch.org/science/2023/02/15/ai-awareness-methodology/`. Pew therefore has a
nationally representative, IRT-scaled knowledge measure of US adults. Anyone who reads the
methodology page will know it.

The claim that survives concerns **what is measured**, not rigour:

- Safe: **no representative measure of what adults can do with AI exists.** Pew's six items ask
  people to identify where AI already works in email, online shopping and customer service.
  Pew calls it "awareness of AI in daily life" and says the items "are not designed to be an
  exhaustive list". Recognising that a spam filter uses AI is not the same as writing a prompt,
  checking a wrong AI answer, or deciding when not to use the tool.
- Safe: one wave, December 2022, no repeat, so no trend.
- Not safe: "no nationally representative scored assessment exists", "no ability scale", "no item
  statistics". All three are false according to the Pew methodology page. Anyone can check them in one click.
  They would end the meeting.

### 3.4 The nearest representative performance instrument is not about AI

OECD PIAAC assesses literacy, numeracy and problem solving through authentic tasks on probability
samples of adults. Cycle 2 (2023) replaced problem solving in technology-rich environments with
adaptive problem solving. NCES states the two domains "cannot be compared". Neither measures
AI literacy. VERIFIED at nces.ed.gov; the OECD page was blocked.

### 3.5 The gap, in one sentence

Adults are measured on skills that are not AI (PIAAC), or on six recognition items once, in 2022
(Pew). Other AI literacy measures ask people about themselves or test
university students. The one performance-based AI assessment in development, PISA 2029 MAIL, tests
15-year-olds. Nobody measures what adults can do with AI on a sample that represents anyone.

## 5. The five talking points

Each point is defensible on 2026-09-02, with the evidence beside it. None contains a score.

### 5.1 EU law asks for AI literacy and does not ask anyone to measure it

Article 4 has applied to providers and deployers since 2 February 2025. Since the Digital Omnibus of
8 July 2026, it has asked them to "support the development of" AI literacy. It states that the obligation
"does not require providers or deployers to guarantee any specific level of AI literacy of any
individual". The Commission's own Q&A says: "Article 4 of the AI Act does not entail an obligation to
measure the knowledge of AI of employees." No EU fine attaches. No harmonised standard covers it.

**Why this is our point and not an objection to us.** A duty with no measure produces
attendance records for trainings nobody evaluates. We ask a different question from
compliance: how would anyone know whether AI literacy in a population is rising or falling?
Nothing in the Article 4 machinery answers that. The Commission does not claim it does.

### 5.2 Every population-scale AI-literacy number in circulation is self-report

MAILS, SNAIL and AILQ are self-assessment questionnaires, according to their authors
(§3.1). The EU digital-skills headline indicator uses self-declared activities.
Eurobarometer AI items measure opinion. Self-perceived competence is a real and
well-measured construct. It is not what a person can do. No published work permits one to be
read from the other.

### 5.3 No representative measure exists of what adults can do with AI

The existing performance-based AI tests use university students (AILIT, AILIT-S). The
representative performance instruments measure something else (PIAAC). PISA 2029 MAIL,
the one AI assessment in development, tests 15-year-olds.

**State the exception in the same breath, with its statistics.** Pew asked 11,004 weighted US
adults six correct-answer items in December 2022. 30% got all six. The mean was 3.7 out of 6.
Cronbach's alpha was 0.83. It used a 2PL IRT model. It is a real scale. It measures awareness of where AI
already appears in consumer products, once, in 2022. It does not ask anyone to use a model, judge an
answer, or decide when to stop trusting one.

Say "no representative measure of adult AI task performance", never "nobody has tested the
public".

### 5.4 We can show the method today, and the method is the part that is checkable

OURS, all of it verifiable in the public repository:

- **The practice keys are published.** `instruments/demo-2026.1` ships 20 discrimination items with
  their keys and rationales in the open, on purpose. It carries no score of record.
- **Scores are recomputable from stored inputs, byte for byte.** `score()` is pure. `runPure`
  (`packages/core/src/purity.ts`) fails the build if it reads a clock, a random number or the
  network. For a score the browser issued, the report recomputes each track in front of the
  candidate. A score issued by the exam service is marked `scoredBy: "server"` and claims no local
  replay.
- **A judge's output is a stored input.** Re-scoring is reproducible. Re-judging is not. We state
  both because no model call may sit on the recompute path.
- **Items are content-addressed.** An edit creates a new item and never mutates one.

An auditor can check every line in the public repository today. The purity harness has
limits, listed in `packages/core/src/purity.ts`. It traps clock, randomness, network and
deferred scheduling at call time. It cannot see a reference captured before the call, a
`node:fs` imported at module load, or a `process.env` read. Byte-identical replay is verified on
the pinned runtime. Cross-runtime identity is not proven.

### 5.5 We have written down what would falsify us, and we publish it first

OURS. `docs/SAMPLING.md` §11 specifies in advance the sentences allowed at each
stage. It forbids the words "national" and "representative" for the open cohort in any language.
`docs/TRANSFER-STUDY.md` §1 records that our practice loop combines the two arms that did

Publishing the list of results that would sink us is cheaper now than receiving it later.

## 6. What we are not claiming

Put this in the pack. Do not save it for the objection.

**Marking, so nobody is misled about what is checkable.** Points 1 to 3 concern systems in
the private backend repository or this repository's history. A reader cannot verify 1 and 2
from the public tree. Say that instead of gesturing at "the repo".

1. **No score of record has been issued.** The `scores` table in the exam service is empty. The
   credential asserts a completed sitting and says nothing about ability (`docs/CREDENTIAL.md`).
2. **The judging pipeline is not in production.** T3 and T4 depend on an LLM judge whose outputs
   are stored and replayed. The pipeline that collects them does not exist yet. Both facts live in
   the private backend repository. Treat them as our statement, not as something you can check
   here.
3. **The operational item bank was public in this repository until 2026-08-30.** Any exposure that
   caused is already in the world. Two tests now fail the build if operational content returns
   (`packages/content-tools/test/public-tree.test.ts`,
   `packages/core/test/frontendOnly.test.ts`). That is a control, not a fix for the past.
4. **The reliance construct has no external validity evidence.** Our signed reliance index is our
   own invention. No published work reports a one-dimensional index of reliance calibration. No
   study reports a reliability coefficient for any behavioural appropriate-reliance measure. The
   one direct test found ICC below 0.5 at 153 trials. Source: the evidence reviews
   `docs/EVIDENCE-CALIBRATED-RELIANCE.md` §8 and
   `docs/EVIDENCE-RELIABILITY-AND-TIME-PRESSURE.md` §A1, both in the **private** backend
   repository and not readable from here.
5. **Judge agreement is not settled.** The QWK 0.708 to 0.712 figure we cite is one unreviewed
   preprint, one dataset, one model family. Human inter-rater median on the same dataset is 0.76.
   The judge is below human agreement, not at it. Source:
   `docs/EVIDENCE-JUDGE-AGREEMENT.md`, also private.
6. **No cross-language or cross-country evidence exists.** No invariance testing, no translated
   forms, no field trial.
7. **We are not neutral, and we should stop using the word.** An examiner is funded by somebody,
   and editorial independence does not cure who chose the donors. `docs/POSITIONING.md` no longer
   calls the empty seat "neutral". It states six governance conditions instead: a diversified
   funding pool with a per-donor cap, no donor veto over methods or publication, an independent
   board with a public conflict register, preregistered methods, mandatory publication of negative
   results, and funding committed before results are known. None of the six is in place.
8. **We have no partnership, endorsement or pilot with any body named in this file.** Every
   organisation in §4 appears because its published documents were read, not because anyone spoke
   to us.

## 8. Three ways this pack goes wrong

- **The AI Board publishes Article 4 recommendations with common objectives.** New Article 4(3)
  requires them. If they appear, §5.1 is out of date that week.
- **Somebody fields a scored adult AI-literacy survey.** Pew already has the panel, the scale and
  the method. A second wave with harder items would close most of the gap in one field period.
  PIAAC Cycle 3 is the slower, larger candidate. Recheck §3 before every meeting.
- **We get asked for our own number.** Say that we have none. Then give
  `docs/SAMPLING.md` §11 Stage 0. A small calibration cohort supports item statistics
  and nothing with a population noun in it.
