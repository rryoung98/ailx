# Pass 2 / Reference and mood

Author: AI in the role of concept artist.
Reviewer: AI in the role of art director, in a separate step. Not human professional review.

No reference image is stored here. Each entry names a body of work that a reader can look
up. Each entry says what it contributes. Nothing here is copied; the Foray aesthetic must be
its own.

## Annotated references

| Reference | What it contributes | What we do not take |
|---|---|---|
| Animal Crossing villagers | One skeleton and one head ratio across every species. Personality comes from a small set of types crossed with species. Coherence of the world comes from the shared rules, not from detail. | The infant eye line, the two-head proportion and the constant smile. Our audience is adult. |
| Dick Bruna's Miffy | Economy of line. Two dots and a cross carry a whole state. Expression through tilt of the head. | Front-only symmetry. We need three-quarter views. |
| Richard Scarry's Busytown | Adults with jobs. Animals doing ordinary tasks with ordinary tools. Nobody is a mascot. | Density of the pages. Our scenes hold one action. |
| Charley Harper's birds | Flat geometric bodies. A wedge for a beak. A bird reduced to four shapes and still that bird. | Total stillness. Ours must act. |
| Alexander Girard's wooden dolls | Painted wood, flat color, adult playfulness. A figure that is clearly a made object. | Fixed limbs. Ours need two wing pivots and a neck. |
| Kokeshi dolls | A head on a body with no neck detail. Paint carries the character. Material honesty. | No limbs at all. |
| Bruno Munari's paper toys | Folded paper as a costume material. One crease as decoration. | Fragility. Costume must survive a 24 px render. |

## Two competing directions

**Direction 1, painted wood toy.** Rigid egg bodies, flat color, few joints, a matte
finish. Clothing is painted on or folded from paper. Expression lives in the face and the
tilt. Strength: coherence with the storyboard's materials and the strongest small-size
read. Weakness: acting range.

**Direction 2, editorial animal.** Line-led figures with more limbs and more costume, in
the manner of a picture-book adult. Expression lives in the whole body. Strength: acting
range and adult tone. Weakness: line detail vanishes at 40 px and the costume invites
clutter.

## Selection

Direction 1 for the body and Direction 2 for the face and the limbs. The body is one
rigid pear. The wings pivot. The face uses lids and brows in the editorial manner.
Costume is one folded-paper garment. This hybrid is the shape language for pass 3.

## Cultural tone

A small town commons, not a school and not an office. Painted wood, paper, ceramic and
brass are all things people repair. The tone is capable and slightly rumpled. Nobody here
is perfect and nobody is a child.

## Addendum, cycle 2

After cycle 1 the product owner asked for a character closer to cozy life-sim games and
gentle animation. Fifteen concepts were generated with an image model as exploration, in
`concepts/`. They served this pass's purpose, competing directions before selection, and
nothing else. See `13-owl-rebuild.md`.

## Finish reference, set by the product owner

On 2026-09-12 the product owner looked at the generated owl concepts and said the
aesthetic was right. That sheet, `concepts/owl-sheet.png`, is the finish reference for
production art. The finish means soft rounded forms and a thin clean line. It means a muted
warm palette on warm off-white, a blush on each cheek, one highlight in each eye, and
folded-paper garments. The
construction sheets in this folder do not attempt that finish. An artist matches it.

## Reviewer notes

Direction 2 alone would repeat the first storyboard's problem: characters that look like a
generic illustration style. Direction 1 alone would be too still for a guide who must show
uncertainty. The hybrid is approved as a working hypothesis. Pass 3 must test it in black.
