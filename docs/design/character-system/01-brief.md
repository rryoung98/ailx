# Pass 1 / Brief

Author: AI in the role of character and narrative designer.
Reviewer: AI in the role of art director. Not human professional review.

## Audience

Adults taking a four to five minute AI literacy test. Many arrive from a friend's shared
result. They did not come for a game. They came for a personal result. The organizer
must make the first minute feel like joining something, not like starting an exam.

## Role in the Commons

The organizer runs the community repair afternoon. They are the person who asked an AI
assistant for help with the invitation. The visitor directs, checks and revises that
help. The organizer is the client of the work, not the teacher and not the judge.

## Desire

They want everyone to feel invited. Every yes feels like a small success to them.

## Contradiction

They commit before they check the practical details. They said yes to forty people and
the room holds twenty. They know this about themselves and it still happens.

## Habits

- They rearrange chairs when nervous. A chair moves a few centimetres, then moves back.
- They hold the seating plan folded, and unfold it only when someone asks a hard question.
- When corrected, they pause, then act. No apology, no praise for the corrector.

## Relationship to the visitor

Side by side, not face to face. They sit at the same table and look at the same paper.
The organizer supplies the goal and the constraints. The visitor supplies judgement.
The organizer never says the visitor was right or wrong. They say what they will do next.

## Purpose in the learning interaction

The organizer gives the eight decisions a reason to exist. The source notes are their
notes. The assistant draft is the draft they asked for. Their reactions acknowledge a
choice without revealing whether it matched the scoring key. A weak result never
disappoints them. This rule comes from `../COMMONS-STORYBOARD.md` and stays fixed.

## What the organizer must never do

- Congratulate the visitor.
- Explain a concept in a speech bubble longer than one line.
- Wince, cheer or otherwise grade an answer with their body.
- Look at the camera during a decision.

## Criteria for evaluating designs

1. Reads as one specific person at 24 px, 64 px and 200 px, in solid black.
2. Reads against the forest green wall in grayscale.
3. Shows five states without captions: listening, uncertainty, overcommitment, reconsideration, satisfaction.
4. Reads as an adult. No infant eye line, no oversized head.
5. Survives removal of the costume and of color.
6. Has one habit a viewer can name after one scene.
7. Can be built from ten or fewer shapes per view.
8. Differs in silhouette family from the loupe companion.

## Reviewer notes

The brief is short enough to check against. Criterion 7 is a construction budget and will
constrain pass 4. Criterion 8 matters because the storyboard already asks whether the loupe
is distinct enough. Accepted with one change: criterion 1 now names solid black, so that
pass 3 cannot pass on line detail.
